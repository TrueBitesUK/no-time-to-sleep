Cold Chain · Food Ecommerce · Logistics Technology

Temperature-controlled delivery for food ecommerce.

We're exploring whether independent food businesses can access refrigerated delivery more efficiently by connecting ecommerce demand with existing temperature-controlled carrier capacity.

I'm a food businessI'm a refrigerated carrier

Early-stage validation. We're speaking to merchants and carriers before building a full platform.

The problem

A cold chain built from boxes and ice packs.

Many smaller chilled and frozen ecommerce businesses rely on normal parcel networks combined with insulated packaging, liners and ice or gel packs.

That adds packaging cost, weight and operational complexity. Delays and failed deliveries become particularly expensive when the parcel contains perishable food.

We're testing whether active refrigerated transport can reduce the total cost and risk of a successfully delivered order.

The model

Not another courier company.

The opportunity is the technology and network layer between food merchants and refrigerated operators that already have vehicles, drivers and capacity.

01

Merchant

A food business receives chilled or frozen ecommerce orders.

02

Platform

Order, postcode, weight, temperature and delivery requirements are combined and routed.

03

Carrier

Deliveries are allocated to suitable refrigerated operators and efficient multi-drop routes.

What it could become

The operational layer between checkout and delivery.

If the economics work, software could manage the complexity that currently sits between merchant and carrier.

Who we're speaking to.

Online butchers, raw pet food, meal prep, seafood, farm shops, frozen-food merchants — and refrigerated operators with multi-drop capability or spare route capacity.
